ABSTRACT   
Through the creation of a polar digital space and the data architecture in it, an attempt will be made to get as close as 
possible to the real possibilities of building a Digital Twin in the Antarctic or the polar region, and also to propose the 
European data policy in the Antarctic and the Arctic.  
The data sources and instruments at many of the Antarctic bases are not interoperable, and this creates a major problem 
for comparison and simulation modeling even on a single island, let alone a larger area or the Antarctic mainland.  
In most of the polar summer, the optical data is not of good quality due to the presence of frequent cloud cover. This 
necessitates the mandatory use of in situ data in order to verify the others.  
The purpose of this type of researc h is to enforce certain standards for data dissemination such as Open Data, Open Science, 
and also to show the dynamics of certain territories on Livingstone Island based on research from the summer of 2022 -
2023 through various methods, indices and applic ations, published on the project page1. The complex approach used in 
the research provides reliable, up -to-date and interoperable information, regardless of the geographical location of the 
objects and is devoid of subjective interference.  
The use of data from a drone, ground data from SEKONIC spectrometer, data from NAVAL RSV 421ship2, satellites isn't 
a new method, but a complex. Using the maximum number of applications and also creating an innovative weather station 
certainly brings even greater added val ue to society and science, which seriously investigates climate change in polar 
latitudes.  
A ground weather station AWG, powered by an environmentally friendly magnesium -air battery, was developed especially 
for a project of the Bulgarian Antarctic Base3. 
The data and models will serve the Bulgarian initiative for the construction of the Digital Twins  in Antarctica and Arctica 
or Digital polar space , initiatives such as Oceans and Seas , which is being pilot developed in the department of Aerospace 
Informati on, Space Research and Technology Institute – Bulgarian Academy of Sciences.  
 
Keywords : Antarctica, Climate changes, Digital polar space, Digital twins, Interoperability, SAR  
 
1. INTRODUCTION  
The purpose of this research does not mandate the use or non -use of particular data. This is an attempt to use some of the 
high-value data that is open access and in machine -readable format for the Antarctic territory.  
Combining them with in situ surveys, drone and satellite data, which are shared data on the Internet a s a good practice, can 
create a digital polar space and support initiatives such as Oceans and Seas, Earth's Digital Twins and vario us types of 
research with local , regional and global significance.  
This research has brought together several use cases that  can be used in Antarctic climate change research for different 
sites of interest.

Data spaces or data digital spaces are data sharing ecosystems which are built on uniform rules, certified data providers 
and recipients and tr ust among partners. They provide a secure and privacy -preserving infrastructure to pool, access, 
process, use and share data. Data owners have control over who has access to their data, for what purpose and under what 
conditions it can be used4.  
The aim of this study is to demonstrate the importance of creating a polar data space that is properly structured and 
interoperable databases. In this way, in addition to contributing to a more effective study of climate change, they will also  
contribute to the con struction of many different models, one of which may be the Earth's digital twin.  
According to the author, the construction of such a space can be upgraded and supplemented, because in addition to 
distance research, there are other fields that actively gen erate data in polar conditions, such as medicine, biology, sociology 
and others.  
The fact is, however, that there are also quite a few differences in data structuring, and this is mainly due to the internat ional 
teams and projects that develop mainly in th e Antarctic region, because in the Arctic and in the European part, the data are 
more clearly defined and correspond of the interoperability requirements.  
In this regard, the European Commission rules and Inspire , Open and Spatial Data Policy could easily be proposed and 
implemented in relation to high -value data5 to which the data from research in the Antarctic during the summer season 
2022 -2023 fall, where a synchronous monitoring approach was made based on remote sensing, Unmanned aerial 
vehicles (UAV’s)  and in situ data.  
Additionally, as hinted by their name, they are especially valuable when it comes to creating value -added services, 
applications and, more generally, high -quality jobs for society a nd the EU6, figure 1. 
The identification and definition of these high -value datasets lead to a significant change in the field of open data, as the 
chosen datasets will be made available in harmonised technical standards. This aims to increase their potential for re use 
and henceforth their impact6. 
These charac teristics help overcome a series of barriers that often restrict the free circulation of information, such as high -
use fees, non -machine -readable content, restrictive licences, poor interoperability or poor accessibility due to scattered 
data holders6.

2. DATA AND METHODS  
2.1. Data acquisition  
Spectral analysis is one of the most important  physical methods for studying objects. It ’s used to determine qualitative and 
quantitative characteristics of objects based on their spectral curve, generated from in situ and satellite data . 
Data from Sentinel -2 by the European Space Agency (ESA) as part  of the Copernicus program  were used . Sentinel -2 is a 
land-monitoring constellation of two identical polar -orbiting satellites —Sentinel -2A and Sentinel 2B —phased at 180° to 
each other. It is equipped with a state -of-the-art high -resolution multispectral im ager (MSI) instrument, which provides a 
290 km swath width and covers all of Earth’s land surfaces, large islands, and inland and coastal waters every five days10.

The simplistic definition would be how hot the "surface" of the Earth would feel to the touch at a certain location. From 
the satellite's perspective, the "surface" is all it sees whe n it looks through the atmosphere at Earth. It can be snow and ice, 
the grass on the lawn, the roof of a building or the leaves in the crown of the forest. LST is not the same as air temperatur e, 
which is included in the daily weather report16.  
The LST re trieval algorithms are accurate to 1 K, especially at night when differential surface heating is absent16.

The high color temperature values of the object that is demonstrated in fig. are 5943 in Kelvin. They  ensure th at the 
research is done in daylight and in clear weather. Also the CRI values are high. A CRI equal to 100 Ra is assumed to be 
daylight (sunlight), and in this particular case it is equal to 99.3 or extremely high. At this value of light, the col ors of the 
observed objects are perceived most naturally. The same applies to the other values of R1 to R15, which for the object in 
the case of sea ice are on average 99. These values are also observed for the other objects.  
The Ra value is the fraction o f light produced by the light source. When measuring the luminous efficiency of a light 
fixture, in this case the Sun, CRI measures all color ranges from R1 to R15, but the RA value measures only the blue -green 
areas of the light fixture or sunlight, R1 to  R8, at that Ra point = 99.3. The most difficult color to reproduce is red (R=9) 
and in the particular example it has the lowest values R9=94.2.

4. CONCLUSION  
Spectral curves have long shown their benefits, as they do not change, but can only  serve to validate research in polar 
regions, as very often research is seasonal, and in winter the sites are difficult to access.  
Seasonal field surveys are used to supplement and enrich information on spectral profiles and libraries for specific sites or  
a group of sites . 
Regardless of the observed objects, it’s desirable first to match data from optical and radar images, because if there is 
specific data that is not visible with one satellite, it can be compensated by a second or third. This necessitates  the creation 
of a data space in which data can be processed, but also processed by different experts, in order to extract maximum 
information about the studied area. And last but not least, that this information is similarly structured so that it can be 
shared and interoperable.  
It turned out that the dynamics of Livingstone Island can be successfully inferred by the three satellites Sentinel 1 SAR, 
Sentinel 2MSI and Sentinel 3LSTR, and also individually, but only with the availability of quality optical r ange data.  
The parameters obtained from Sentinel 3LSTR are absolutely sufficient for the initial needs of building polar space data 
for initiatives such as Digital Twins, Oceans and Seas with an average terrain resolution of 1 km, but periodically it will 
be good to verify and with data from the other two satellites, and why not using more satellites like Sentinel 5P.  
The four hybrid remote sensing monitoring models thus constructed could be successfully applied to the various needs, as 
they have already be en proven to work through a synchronous approach during the 31st Bulgarian Antarctic Expedition.  
With the models thus distinguished, we could partially support the structuring of the data on specific topics or objects of 
interest.  
And since there is also d ata and research from previous years, which are based on open data from the Copernicus program8, 
the following conclusions can be drawn:  
1. The dynamics of temperature change from 2018 to 2022 are clearly visible, and for this purpose, many more images 
than the ones presented were used.  
2. In addition to the temperature, the changes in the presence of snow cover from Sentinel 2, the retreat of the ice cover, 
and the lack of sea ice characteristic of the area are also clearly observed.  
3. All spectral indice s can be successfully applied when there is a lack of snow cover and rock exposure. There, the spectral 
profiles show a 100% match between in situ and satellite data. This helps the good study of cracks in the rocks, which are 
additionally verified with th e radar data, and where there are ground points, it is even recommended to use them together 
with drone footage and photogrammetric models . 
4. SAR continues to be the most accurate indicator when selecting data, but Sentinel 3 gives us daily information on  climate 
change, which also gives enough additional parameters  
5. From the conducted research and what has been seen on the ground, it can be concluded that the territory of BAB, Cape 
Hanna Point and large areas of the western part of the island in the las t few years have been formed as local small heat 
islands for Antarctica.  
As for Hanna Point due to orographic reasons and the accumulation of large colonies of polar species of animals, this 
phenomenon has been observed in previous years and is also visibl e in the large amount of vegetation such as mosses, 
lichens and grasses.
